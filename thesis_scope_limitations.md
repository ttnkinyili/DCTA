# Scope and Limitations of the Study

## 1.1 Research Boundaries

This research investigates the mathematical foundations of continuous trust evaluation within Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA) for heterogeneous enterprise networks. Neither NIST SP 800-207 nor the Cloud Security Alliance's SDP Specification v2.0 prescribes a rigorous mechanism for computing, decaying, or fusing trust signals across multiple telemetry domains over the lifetime of a session — leaving the implicit trust period structurally unresolved (Rose et al., 2020; Cloud Security Alliance, 2024). The thesis addresses this gap through six progressively sophisticated trust models, culminating in an Ensemble Trust Model that hybridises Dempster-Shafer (DS) evidential fusion with dual-horizon exponential temporal decay. The investigative perimeter prioritises algorithmic rigour over vendor-specific deployment and is bounded as follows.

The study targets the Policy Decision Point (PDP), the algorithmic layer where continuous trust scores are computed. The enforcement layer is treated as an interchangeable substrate, validated here through Open Policy Agent and Envoy Proxy but deliberately decoupled from any commercial product so that the scientifically generalisable contribution — how evidence is weighted, fused, and temporally discounted — remains independent of deployment-specific engineering concerns. Trust evaluation spans four independent domains — Identity, Device, Network, and Application/Data — aligned with the NIST SP 800-207 pillars, with binary compliance facets such as MFA completion, patch currency, TLS version, and data classification match modelled as Bernoulli observations at the lowest tier of the probabilistic pipeline. Additional domains that may arise in specialised contexts, including physical access telemetry, wearable biometrics, and supply-chain provenance, are excluded from the current evaluation, though the Dempster-Shafer combination rule accommodates an arbitrary number of evidence sources and the framework is therefore mathematically extensible without structural modification.

The evidential framework employs classical DS theory under the Closed World Assumption with Dempster's rule as the sole fusion operator (Shafer, 1976). Alternative combination rules, including Yager's rule, the Proportional Conflict Redistribution family, and Dezert-Smarandache Theory, are acknowledged in the literature review but are not empirically compared within the simulation framework. Temporal decay is restricted to linear and exponential formulations; logistic, hyperbolic, or machine-learned decay profiles are identified as future research directions. The Bernoulli-Binomial hierarchical model assumes statistical independence among facets within each domain, an assumption that is operationally reasonable for the facets selected but is not guaranteed in deployment contexts where correlated failures — such as a system compromise simultaneously disabling endpoint protection and subverting hardware attestation — may produce underestimated domain-level variance.

Validation proceeds through algorithmic simulation and testbed emulation across six threat scenarios — Corporate Office, Remote VPN, Public Wi-Fi, BYOD, Untrusted Device with Geofence Violation, and Compromised Endpoint — rather than production-scale deployment. The containerised testbed integrating Docker and LXC containers, Mininet-emulated SDN topology, Keycloak, OpenDaylight, OPA, and Envoy demonstrates architectural feasibility at 25 active nodes with 2.1 ms policy evaluation latencies but does not replicate the full scale, concurrency, or environmental noise of a production enterprise network comprising thousands of endpoints.

## 1.7 Limitations

While this research establishes a rigorous mathematical foundation for dynamic trust computation and demonstrates its feasibility through systematic emulation, several interrelated limitations constrain the generalisability of the findings. These limitations are acknowledged transparently to provide a balanced assessment of the research contributions and to delineate the natural boundaries for future investigation.

### 1.7.1 Validation Environment Constraints

The synthetic telemetry employed throughout the evaluation, though carefully parameterised against documented threat actor behaviours including credential relay, session hijacking, lateral movement, and low-and-slow exfiltration, inevitably abstracts away the stochastic complexity of production network traffic. Real-world telemetry exhibits non-stationary distributions, correlated cross-domain failures such as infrastructure outages that simultaneously degrade device, network, and application signals, and adversarial behaviours that are inherently unpredictable and evolve in response to deployed defences. The simulation framework's independence assumption across domain telemetry generation means that coordinated multi-domain adversarial manipulation — where an attacker simultaneously spoofs identity, device, and network signals while maintaining stable variance to evade the weighting discount — is not exhaustively tested. The reported detection latencies, false-positive rates, and effective session TTLs consequently require validation with operational datasets before they can be generalised to production environments.

The testbed itself operates at a scale that is orders of magnitude smaller than enterprise deployments. With 25 active nodes and a single controller instance, the environment does not expose scalability bottlenecks such as controller CPU saturation, policy evaluation throughput under concurrent load, and proxy connection handling at tens-of-thousands of simultaneous sessions. The Mininet-emulated network further introduces idealised link characteristics — zero packet loss and deterministic latency — that diverge materially from the variable conditions of production WANs, cellular backhaul, and satellite links, rendering the reported 2.1 ms policy evaluation latency a lower-bound estimate. Furthermore, the containerised environment lacks the fidelity of full hardware virtualisation and Trusted Execution Environments that would be necessary to capture OS-specific vulnerabilities and hardware-level interactions affecting trust evaluation in production deployment (Chou et al., 2025).

### 1.7.2 Security Assumptions and Adversarial Resilience

The model assumes telemetry integrity without integrating hardware trust anchors. Trusted Platform Modules, secure enclaves, and hardware attestation chains are absent from the framework, leaving the system vulnerable to sensor spoofing attacks where a compromised device consistently reports fabricated but stable telemetry. Because the variance-based weighting mechanism treats signal stability as a proxy for reliability, such fabricated telemetry would evade the weighting discount that is designed to suppress erratic or untrustworthy sources. This absence limits the architecture's applicability in high-assurance environments — classified systems, critical infrastructure, and financial services — where hardware-rooted trust is mandated by regulatory or operational policy.

The research does not conduct a formal adversarial robustness analysis against an intelligent adversary who possesses knowledge of the trust computation algorithm and deliberately crafts inputs to maximise evasion probability. Unexplored attack vectors include data poisoning of the variance estimator to manipulate domain weights, gradient-based perturbations of domain scores designed to maintain trust below the re-authentication threshold while evading detection, collusion attacks where multiple compromised domains coordinate fabricated corroborating evidence, and AI-generated adversarial telemetry that preserves stable variance signatures while masking malicious activity. While the evaluation captures credential theft, lateral movement, and insider threats, it does not exhaustively model advanced persistent threat evasion techniques or the evolutionary adaptation of attackers to the specific defences deployed (Ali et al., 2024).

The exclusive use of Dempster's rule of combination introduces an additional constraint. The rule can produce counterintuitive fused beliefs when combining highly conflicting evidence sources, as the normalisation constant amplifies residual mass when the conflict coefficient approaches unity (Zadeh, 1979). The thesis mitigates this through variance-based weight suppression, whereby domains exhibiting high conflict contribute elevated uncertainty rather than committed mass, but it does not formally compare this mitigation against alternative fusion operators such as Yager's disjunctive combination, the PCR5/PCR6 redistribution rules, or Smets's unnormalised Transferable Belief Model. Alternative rules may offer superior robustness in adversarial settings where an attacker deliberately engineers inter-domain conflict to exploit Dempster's normalisation behaviour.

### 1.7.3 Model Scope and Parameter Constraints

The Ensemble Trust Model relies on static parameterisation that limits its adaptability to changing threat conditions. The short-term decay constant, the long-term inertia coefficient, and the variance sensitivity parameter are fixed throughout all simulations, calibrated to compliance baselines drawn from NIST SP 800-63B and PCI DSS v4.0 (Grassi et al., 2017). The research does not implement adaptive parameter tuning, in which decay rates and sensitivity thresholds would be dynamically adjusted based on prevailing threat intelligence, observed entity volatility, or historical model performance. Consequently, the sensitivity of the Ensemble Trust Model to sub-optimal parameter choices — and the conditions under which specific parametrisations produce oscillatory trust behaviour or excessive false positives — has not been characterised across the full parameter space. Reinforcement learning and Bayesian optimisation are identified as promising approaches for adaptive calibration but are deferred to future work.

The model also relies exclusively on direct telemetry, excluding indirect trust propagation through networks such as recommendations from trusted peers, blockchain-based reputation systems, and transitive trust chains. This limitation is particularly significant in federated environments and cross-organisational collaborations where direct observation of an entity may be sparse or entirely unavailable. The associated challenge of evaluating the provenance, age, and mathematical confidence of transmitted trust evidence before incorporating it into the fusion engine remains unaddressed.

### 1.7.4 Applicability and Generalisability Constraints

The architecture assumes a baseline level of network modernisation that supports SDP or SDN agents. Integration with air-gapped systems, industrial control systems governed by safety-critical availability requirements, highly constrained IoT devices lacking computational resources for even lightweight Single Packet Authorisation implementations, and legacy systems predating modern identity frameworks remains unexplored. The Gateway-to-Gateway deployment model, while acknowledged as a potential bridge for accommodating such environments, is not validated within the testbed.

Algorithmic fairness presents an additional dimension that the research identifies but does not empirically address. The variance-based dynamic weighting mechanism treats all facets as neutral technical signals, yet certain Identity-domain facets — particularly behavioural biometrics and access pattern normality metrics — may exhibit differential baseline distributions across user populations. A user with a disability affecting keystroke dynamics, a user in a time zone that produces access patterns flagged as anomalous relative to organisational norms, or a user whose role legitimately requires unusual data access patterns may each receive systematically suppressed trust scores under the current weighting scheme. The thesis identifies fairness and Explainable AI integration as critical operational requirements for production deployment (Chen & Wang, 2025) but does not itself conduct empirical bias auditing across demographic or operational subgroups.

User behaviour realism constitutes a final constraint on generalisability. The evaluation relies on scripted scenarios rather than actual user activity logs, and real-world behaviour exhibits temporal autocorrelations that may be mistaken for anomalies, context-dependent variability between frequent travellers and office-based users, gradual behavioural drift over career progression, and legitimate exceptions during emergencies or off-hours work. The model's capacity to discriminate between genuinely malicious anomalies and benign behavioural variation — particularly in the grey-area trust region between full access and denial thresholds — requires validation with longitudinal real user telemetry.

Several areas, while relevant to operational Zero Trust deployment, are explicitly excluded from this research. The security of the underlying cryptographic primitives — TLS, Single Packet Authorisation, and HMAC — is assumed rather than formally evaluated, and post-quantum cryptographic migration is discussed as a future direction but is not implemented. Formal user experience measurement is not conducted; while the thesis argues that the Ensemble Model reduces false-positive lockouts relative to aggressive exponential decay, no quantitative usability metrics such as System Usability Scale scores, task completion times, or re-authentication frequency distributions are collected. Although trust thresholds are informed by NIST SP 800-63B Authenticator Assurance Levels and PCI DSS v4.0 session timeout requirements, the thesis does not conduct a comprehensive compliance gap analysis against specific regulatory frameworks such as GDPR, HIPAA, or FedRAMP. Finally, the trust model evaluates single-organisation trust computation; federated trust across organisational boundaries, where trust scores would need to be exchanged between independent policy engines operating with potentially divergent weighting schemas and risk appetites, is not addressed.
