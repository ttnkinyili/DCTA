# Thesis Abstract

## Evaluating Zero Trust Models Through Evidential Fusion and Temporal Dynamics in Heterogeneous Enterprise Networks

The persistence of implicit trust periods within contemporary Zero Trust Architecture (ZTA) implementations remains a critical vulnerability in heterogeneous enterprise networks. Despite the proliferation of identity-centric frameworks such as NIST SP 800-207 and the Cloud Security Alliance's Software-Defined Perimeter (SDP) Specification v2.0, current deployments continue to treat authentication as a discrete, binary event that confers durable session access — creating an operational runway for advanced persistent threats, session hijacking, and low-and-slow insider exfiltration. This thesis addresses this deficiency by developing and empirically evaluating a progression of six computational trust models, advancing from static, perimeter-based access control through multi-domain evidential fusion to a novel Ensemble Trust Model grounded in Dempster-Shafer (DS) theory of evidence.

The proposed architecture formally models trust as a continuously depreciating, probabilistically computed quantity. Individual security telemetry facets across four independent domains — Identity, Device, Network, and Application — are modelled as Bernoulli random variables whose binomial proportions feed a variance-based dynamic weighting mechanism. The resulting weights govern the allocation of Dempster-Shafer mass functions between committed belief and epistemic uncertainty, enabling explicit mathematical representation of incomplete or conflicting evidence. The Ensemble Trust Model hybridises short-term data freshness, governed by a 30-minute exponential decay window, with long-term behavioural inertia over a 48-hour horizon, balancing aggressive session ephemerality with operational continuity.

Validation was conducted across six heterogeneous threat scenarios — including corporate office, remote VPN, public Wi-Fi, BYOD, untrusted device with geofence violation, and compromised endpoint — using a containerised testbed integrating OpenDaylight, Keycloak, Open Policy Agent, and Envoy over a Mininet-emulated SDN fabric. Results demonstrate that the Ensemble Model eliminates the implicit trust period, achieves contextual gray-area routing that reduces false-positive lockouts, and enforces proportional session degradation, providing a mathematically rigorous blueprint for Continuous Adaptive Risk and Trust Assessment (CARTA) in heterogeneous enterprise environments.
